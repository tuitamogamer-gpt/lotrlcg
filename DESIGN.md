# There & Back Again

The original Fantasy Flight Games LOTR LCG is the visual reference across the entire interface: printed serif text, ivory card stock, botanical ornament, charcoal framing, worn brass, and forest green. The adventure atlas and physical tabletop share that language. Original generated scenery and an unlabelled parchment map carry the atmosphere; card scans retain their original attribution.

Paper #eee8d8, ink #302d25, charcoal #1f2522, brass #846b36, forest #485b42. Self-hosted IM Fell English SC supplies printed small-cap titles; Crimson Pro supplies interface text, rules, italic narrative, and lining/tabular counters. These OFL-licensed alternatives evoke the original game's type without claiming to be its exact lettering. Latin Extended body fonts cover names such as Glóin and Éowyn. Thin double borders, original vinework, asymmetrical frame corners, and quiet transitions carry the component aesthetic through menus, controls, and dialogs. Phosphor icons and reduced-motion support remain. All labels, card rules, and controls are live HTML.

The adventure page reads like an open chronicle: assemble 1–3 heroes, choose normal or campaign play, then choose a quest. Every hot-seat hero shows their own starter deck. Classic solo is a separate, explicit choice. Large original forest, river, and fortress scenes distinguish the three Core Set quests.

During play, the compact masthead sits above a continuous illustrated cloth playmat with a stitched edge and a wooden rim. The journey rail holds the landscape quest card, progress tokens, and active location. Shared staging and engaged enemies occupy the far side; the fellowship and neighboring seats sit nearer the player. Separate encounter and player decks with face-up discards sit at the right. Full card scans, quarter-turn exhaustion, tucked attachments, facedown shadows, physical tokens, and a two-wheel threat counter follow tabletop conventions. The hand stays along the near edge, beside the turn controls. Card sizes adapt to each zone, and crowded zones scroll internally. Hero banners and number keys switch seats.

Names, costs, resource pools, statistics, and legal play controls remain visible without relying on miniature scans. Hover for a large preview; click for full rules and contextual play. Separate discard piles, sortable hands, playable filtering, quest forecasts, cooperative support controls, and explicit phase handoffs reduce repeated navigation. Pending decisions identify the hero resolving them.

Live statistics use original vector reliefs with engraved brass bezels and colored enamel: a silver feather for willpower, a steel sword for attack, a blue shield for defense, a ruby heart for hit points, an amber Eye for threat, and a silver tree for location progress. Shared badges use 28px symbols and 19px live values on the table, larger labelled readings in the inspector, and compact three-column comparisons in combat decisions. Wounded HP has a distinct ruby frame as well as an accessible remaining-health label. SVG paint IDs are unique per instance for Safari; decorative artwork never owns or changes game state.

The turn panel carries a coaching lamp: one plain-language suggestion for the current decision, computed from the visible table, never acting on the player's behalf. Event reviews follow the chosen review mode, so the table only interrupts for hidden information and losses unless the player asks for every event. The deck builder and journey record use the same paper, ink and brass language as the library.

Desktop and mobile share the same live controls. The adventure archive, builder, library and navigation are checked from 320px phones through desktop windows; larger tabletop views retain their existing layout. Focus handling, labelled buttons, keyboard shortcut guards, comfortable/compact density, and reduced-motion preferences remain part of the interface.

Physical-game research, reference links, and the digital adaptations are recorded in [TABLETOP-DESIGN-RESEARCH.md](docs/TABLETOP-DESIGN-RESEARCH.md).

The collector’s table adds three original imagegen playmats: Mirkwood woodland, the Anduin at dawn, and the shadowed ruins of Dol Guldur. A stitched perimeter and walnut rim frame the art; translucent journey, deck, and hand areas preserve readable controls. The surface follows the current quest by default, with a persistent manual choice in table preferences. Gilded White Tree and copper Eye card backs are shared by the decks, facedown shadows, and reveal animation. Gold resource and jade progress artwork carry live counts; damage uses the original red-and-black printed wound token with an accessible number overlay. Artwork prompts and production details are recorded in [PREMIUM-TABLE.md](docs/PREMIUM-TABLE.md).

Readability uses a shared 14px minimum caption token across comfortable, compact and responsive layouts. Descriptions and actions use 16–18px Crimson Pro, with 20px rules text in the card inspector. Darker ink on parchment and stronger forest backings on the illustrated table keep text separate from artwork. At short laptop heights, card names and live statistics sit beside the scans, leaving the hand anchored and readable. The primary turn action precedes the phase explanation so long descriptions cannot push it out of reach. The Adventures page includes a visible fan-project notice and direct links to Fantasy Flight Games, the Revised Core Set Learn to Play, and Rules Reference PDFs.

Below the fellowship setup, the quest choice reads as a second chapter of the same page: a brass kicker and small-cap heading beside the normal/campaign tabs, then a table of contents on card stock next to the adventure panel. The contents list every automated quest in chapter order, grouped under sticky release headings (Core Set, Shadows of Mirkwood, Dwarrowdelf, Against the Shadow), each row carrying its numeral in a brass pill, the printed name, a one-line tagline and the difficulty. The list scrolls inside its own frame, so the adventure panel sets the row height and the page keeps the same length however many quests are registered; the chosen chapter is brought into view without moving the page, and campaign play greys out every chapter but the current one. The adventure panel titles every quest in two lines with an emphasised destination, and reuses the three original sceneries by setting: the river for the Anduin valley and Gondor’s waterways, the fortress for Moria, Dol Guldur and besieged cities, the forest for the rest.

The landing opens with a full-width Mirkwood illustration and the oversized “The road is calling” title. The landscape, translucent mist and foreground botanical silhouettes move at different depths with scroll; a gentle spring follows the mouse. The warm brass primary action scrolls to and focuses the adventure archive, or resumes the selected saved journey. A three-step navigation connects fellowship setup, quest selection and the final preview. A secondary action opens the guide. Reduced-motion preference disables all hero movement, and ambient animation pauses offscreen. The original game attribution and three official resource links follow the adventure content. The existing artwork and font files are reused.

The builder keeps the supported collection within reach: a searchable hero gallery retains selected heroes, while card results open 48 at a time and expand on request. Search throughout the builder, library, adventure archive and published scenario lists accepts names with or without accents. A mobile progress bar links directly to the deck summary; the desktop summary stays below the masthead. Library filters can be cleared without leaving the current category, and scenario details remain available only while their recipe matches the visible filters.

Page navigation moves keyboard focus to the loaded main content, including lazy routes; dismissing the mobile menu with Escape returns to its opener. Active navigation items expose the current page. If saving an adventure fails, a persistent notice offers an export of the latest in-memory progress. It remains visible until a subsequent save succeeds.

Deck editing separates the full supported collection from the cards already in the working deck. Both views share search, sphere, release and card-type filters, and expose live design and copy counts. Removing the last copy keeps keyboard focus in the card browser. Copying a saved custom deck opens a new working copy with its own identity; the original remains unchanged until the player explicitly saves a separate deck.

The journey record opens with recent adventures and their dates. Quest totals initially show only played adventures, with a toggle for the full quest list. Labelled keyboard-accessible scroll frames contain both result tables on small screens.

How to play reads as one bound chapter in a single 1120px column: each note keeps its heading in the left margin beside the text, the seven phases sit on numbered paper cards with an engraved icon, and source links use dark ink rather than the old pale gold. Fellowship cards in the setup gallery keep their own height, so short learning decks no longer carry the empty space of the longer Collector's Edition notes. The parchment atlas behind every page covers the full viewport on tall screens.
