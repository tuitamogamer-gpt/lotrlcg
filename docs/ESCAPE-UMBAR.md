# Escape from Umbar

The original Sands of Harad scenario supports standard and easy play for one to four local players. The immutable catalog and original recipe snapshot remain reference evidence; the gameplay recipe incorporates printed inventory corrections recorded in HARAD-SOURCES.md.

The main quest has two 15-point stages. Its printed ceiling applies to ordinary questing and progress placed directly on the main quest, including Umbar Square and Haradrim Estate. An engaged enemy belonging to any player prevents advancing. Stage 2 resolves the end of the entire current phase, including temporary-ally departures and discarding current and suspended combat shadows, then shuffles the encounter discard pile into the deck, then finds and engages one enemy per player in first-player order. Its beginning-of-quest trigger discards for exactly one additional staging enemy when at least one player has no engaged enemy.

“Seize them!” remembers the round in which the current main stage entered play. Zero main-quest progress loses the game only after a round that the stage spent entirely in play. Actual combat destruction of a character removes main-quest progress using the attacker's modified threat; side-quest progress is independent.

Southron Champion retains the physical side quest and the physical enemy chosen by its discard search. The Champion receives its resource token, +2 threat, attack and defense and immunity while that side quest remains active. Fewer than four progress on the side quest prevents damage; neither the main quest nor that side quest can be defeated while the Champion remains in play. Completing four progress unlocks damage without prematurely discarding the side quest.

Narrow Alleyway offers its optional response only after an actual travel. The chosen non-unique engaged enemy leaves play, losing damage, resources, attachments and shadow cards. Its real physical identity remains faceup under that location, outside all in-play targeting and combat lists. When the location leaves play, the physical card reenters engaged with the first player before excess quest progress checks advancement. A queued advancement also rechecks the engaged-enemy restriction. This held zone is serialized and validated separately from the board.

Each location implements its printed travel requirement: Umbar Square reveals an encounter card, Port of Harad assigns damage among any players' characters, and Haradrim Estate searches the encounter deck and discard pile for a Harad enemy, engages the first player, places progress directly on the main quest, and shuffles. Estate rejects staging-area progress. Port uses the total number of engaged enemies as X, and Umbar Street uses actual main-quest progress for engagement modifiers.

Harad Soldiers and Harad Territory are shared with Desert Crossing. Captain bonuses distinguish staging-area threat from the engaged player's other Harad enemies. Soldier and Archer engagement effects preserve player ownership. Soldier's shadow can select only a non-unique staged enemy, matching the printed card. In Enemy Territory enforces each player's private five-card hand limit immediately and offers the printed draw response when defeated. Enfeebled prevents readying exhausted characters through the end of combat; its shadow lasts until the round ends. Injured Companion and Nowhere to Hide shadows retain actual attack and engagement history across saves.

The 36 focused scenario tests pass and cover all player counts and both difficulty modes, the progress ceiling and advancement locks, stage transition timing, loss timing, the Champion, Alleyway physical conservation and reload, travel, shared continuous effects, hand privacy, engagement shadows, and malformed saves. Broader simulation and browser results are recorded by the release validation workflow.
