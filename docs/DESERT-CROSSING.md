# Desert Crossing

Original _The Sands of Harad_ scenario, standard and easy mode, for one to four local players. The scenario uses the Desert Crossing, Desert Sands and Desert Creatures encounter sets. Original difficulty: 6.

The immutable source is `src/data/pending/desert-crossing-import.json`, with runtime records in `src/data/harad-{encounter-cards,quest-cards,recipes}.json`. Printed original scans and Fantasy Flight's [MEC55 rules](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-fantasyflightgames/uploads/2026/09/mec55_rules_webready.pdf) establish the rules. The PDF is 3,678,999 bytes, SHA-256 `dda538cf6c73514cdab24f2df02ddfc05e6cd3ee50d9d31f147e6858b84f2ba4`.

There are 20 original card identities: three double-sided quests and 17 encounter designs, represented by 23 printed faces. The runtime standard recipe has 37 physical encounter cards plus three quests; easy mode has 31 plus three. The four Burnt Sands copies are distributed between staging and the encounter deck by the printed one-per-player setup search. Both original Unbearable Heat copies remain in easy mode; the reference recipe incorrectly removes one.

The shared heat tracker starts at 10. Temperature reaches 60 causes immediate loss before later queued effects. The stages require 8, 14 and 16 progress. Each stage raises heat by four at round end; stage two subsequently assigns damage using the new tens digit. Stage changes raise heat by two. Stage two shuffles the encounter discard pile into the deck and reveals one card per player. Stage three lets each player search the encounter deck, discard pile and victory display for one actual Were-worm; progress cannot defeat the final stage while any Were-worm remains in play, including enemies engaged with another player. Were-worm itself has no printed victory points, but player effects can put it into the victory display.

All encounter text, travel triggers, side quest passives and responses, and printed shadows are implemented. Were-worm has printed X threat, attack and defense, and Scorched Hamada has X threat. These update with heat. Carrion Bird counts damaged characters across all fellowships. Parched Wadi modifiers stack. Sand Viper cannot be defended and changes engagement to the first player after actual travel. Becoming active through Mirage resolves location entry effects without counting as travel. Mirage's own physical treachery is held outside the discard pile until its resolution finishes, so its shuffle cannot include itself.

Find Water blocks readying from player card effects while allowing framework refresh and encounter responses. Giant Scorpion poison blocks all readying until the round ends, including framework refresh. Its shadow follows the defending character when attack damage is redirected. Craft Remedy blanks damaged characters' printed text while preserving traits. Towering Dunes blocks progress on other locations in staging while allowing progress on itself and active locations. Side quest and Oasis responses are optional and retain actual player choices. Damage and healing assignments can be distributed among the printed eligible characters.

Two implementation interpretations are explicit, rather than attributed to a designer ruling: Heat Exhaustion can select an already exhausted character because its separate damage instruction still changes the game state; the stage-one ally entry either/or requires the damage option when the ally is already exhausted. Simultaneous round-end Heat and Seek Shade triggers can be ordered by the first player, and each uses the actual temperature when it resolves. No card-specific designer ruling was located for these cases.

Serialized scenario state preserves heat, quest order, resolving Mirage cards and round-limited poison. Queued decisions carry the relevant player for local hot-seat privacy. All 34 focused checks in `tests/desert-crossing.test.ts` pass, including the eight setup combinations, forced ally entry timing and redirected Scorpion shadow damage. Complete-game and browser validation are recorded in the release evidence after integration.
